# Light Leak

The warm fog that creeps in from one edge when a film camera's gate lets
light past: orange, red and yellow blobs shaped by slow noise, drifting
gently. Over transparent; stack it on a clip and it reads as a screen.

- **Intensity** — 0 off, 1 full; above 1 burns the edge out.
- **Hue Shift** — turns the warm palette; ±180 for a cold cyan leak.
- **Scale** — blob size; 1 is a frame-sized leak, 0.5 a tight corner burn.
- **Speed** — drift rate; 0 holds a single still leak.
- **Angle** — which edge the light comes from; 0 is the right side.

## Use it when

- A sequence wants the memory, summer or analogue feel: one leak on a
  separate layer, Intensity 0.5, kept to the transitions and the beats.
- A cut needs cover: ride Intensity up over the cut and the leak becomes a
  soft flash transition.
- Stills and slow pans: the drift gives a locked-off frame a little life.

## Avoid when

- On every clip of the project; a leak is a mistake the camera made once,
  and the look dies the moment it is predictable.
- Faces at the leak edge: the red blob sits on skin and reads as a rash.
- Corporate, medical or product work where the brief is clean; the warm
  haze lowers contrast on exactly the part of the frame the subject is in.
