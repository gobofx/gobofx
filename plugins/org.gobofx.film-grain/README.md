# Film Grain

Animated grain, renewed every frame, heavier in the shadows and midtones
like a film stock.

- **Amount**, **Size** (pixels), **Color Grain** (per-channel), **Shadows**.

## Use it when

- Unifying footage from different cameras and codecs so it reads as one
  stock. Put it on the track, not the clips.
- Giving life back to a flat, clean image, especially after noise reduction.
- Matching a graphic or a composite to the grainy footage around it.

## Avoid when

- Footage that already has grain. Two grains do not add up to one stock;
  they add up to noise.
- Heavily compressed delivery. Grain eats bitrate and turns to mush.
- You want the whole worn-print look. Use Old Film. And at 4K keep **Size**
  near 1, or it stops reading as grain.
