# Variable Blur

Sharp at one point and softer the farther from it you look: the blur
radius ramps from nothing inside the inner radius to its maximum at the
outer one. A radial focus pull rather than a vignette. Max Blur 0 is a
no-op.

- **Center** — the sharp spot; drag the onscreen handle onto the subject.
- **Inner Radius** — how far from the centre stays fully sharp, in frame heights.
- **Outer Radius** — where the blur reaches its maximum, in frame heights.
- **Max Blur** — the blur radius in pixels at and beyond the outer radius (up to 48).

## Use it when

- Holding the eye on a face or a product in a busy frame, with the fall-off
  reading as depth rather than as a mask.
- A still, a drone shot or a flat graphic that needs implied focus.
- A soft vignette of focus on an interview or a portrait, where a dark
  vignette would be too heavy.

## Avoid when

- The sharp zone is a strip across the frame (a road, a skyline): Gradient
  Blur.
- The edge of the sharp region should be a visible disc: Circle Blur.
- The subject moves: keyframe the centre or the fall-off reads as the
  subject slipping out of focus.
