# Circle Screen

The picture as a printed screen of concentric rings instead of a dot grid:
ink on paper, every ring centred on one point, its width swelling where the
picture is dark and thinning to nothing where it is light. **Amount** 0 is
the untouched source.

- **Center** — where the rings radiate from; drag it onto the subject or off-frame for a sweeping arc.
- **Ring Pitch** — spacing between rings in pixels; 6–10 px reads as print, 20+ as a graphic.
- **Ink** — the ring colour.
- **Paper** — the colour between the rings.
- **Amount** — 0 is the source, 1 the full screen; in between the picture shows through the print.

## Use it when

- A pop-art or op-art frame is the brief: rings centred on a face or a logo give a target the eye cannot leave.
- A title bed or an end card wants print texture with a focal point, instead of the flat halftone grid.
- A stylised cutaway (a memory, a hypnosis beat, a radio broadcast) needs to be unmistakably not the footage.

## Avoid when

- Fine detail or text must survive: anything smaller than the pitch dissolves into the rings. Lower **Ring Pitch** or use Posterize.
- The frame is scaled or watched small: rings alias into moiré at the wrong size.
- You want the classic newsprint dot; that is Halftone, and a line screen with a direction is Hatched Screen.
