# Triangle Tile

The plane tiled with equilateral triangles that are all reflections of one
another: a large triangle of the source is read from around the frame
centre and every tile shows it mirrored across its edges, so the pattern is
seamless, three-fold and reads as a kaleidoscope laid flat. There is no
neutral setting; it is always a pattern.

- **Size** — tile side as a fraction of the frame height (0.08 to 0.6); the source triangle always spans most of the frame, so smaller tiles mean more copies.
- **Angle** — rotation of the pattern in degrees; animate it and the lattice turns.

## Use it when

- Geometric backgrounds, bumpers and idents built from the shot's own
  colours.
- Honeycomb, quilt and mosaic motifs: six tiles meet at every vertex as a
  hexagon.
- A psychedelic passage that should feel crystalline rather than swirling;
  pair it with a slow angle animation.

## Avoid when

- Square tiles are wanted, or a tile that shows the whole picture: that is
  Tile or Kaleidoscope Tile.
- The subject must stay recognisable: only a triangle around the centre is
  used, and it is folded six ways at every vertex.
- A single radial fold around one point: Kaleidoscope.
