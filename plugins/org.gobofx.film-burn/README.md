# Film Burn

A frame catching fire in the gate, over transparent. The burn eats in from
one edge: past the front the film is gone and the lamp shows white, at the
front it glows orange over a charred rim, and the edge boils with time.

- **Amount** — how far the burn has eaten in; 0 nothing, 1 the whole frame.
  Keyframe it from 0 to 1 for the classic end-of-reel.
- **Edge Glow** — width of the glowing band.
- **Speed** — how fast the front boils; 0 freezes it.
- **Color** — the glow; orange by default, red for a slower burn.
- **Angle** — the edge it starts from; 0 is the right.

## Use it when

- A scene ends and the cut should feel like the reel ran out: Amount 0 to 1
  over a second, then cut to white or the next shot.
- A flashback or found-footage sequence needs a sting; a short burn at the
  head of a shot says "old print".
- A transition wants heat: pair it with a hot white flash on the next clip.

## Avoid when

- The project is digital and clean; a burn is period and says so loudly.
- Repeated on several cuts; the second burn reads as a template.
- Mid-shot without a cut following it; a burn that stops and recovers has
  no analogue, it looks like a glitch.
