# Hex Lens

The frame seen through a compound eye: a honeycomb of hexagonal cells, each
a small lens magnifying the picture under it, with a faint line at every
facet joint. At Lens Strength 0 the picture passes through unchanged. Pixels
pulled from outside the frame repeat the edge.

- **Cell Size** — centre to centre, in frame heights; 0.12 is about eight cells across a 16:9 frame.
- **Lens Strength** — 0 is a no-op, 1 a full bulge in every cell.

## Use it when

- An insect, drone or machine point of view that should read in one frame.
- A graphic texture over a title card or a backing plate, large cells, low
  strength, under something sharper.
- A keyframed hit: strength from 0 and back as the picture snaps into focus.

## Avoid when

- Dialogue or any shot where a face has to be read; the facets chop it into
  pieces at every strength.
- A single lens is the idea: use Bulge or Ring Lens.
- You want pixelation rather than refraction: Mosaic and Pixellate average
  the cell, this one bends it.
