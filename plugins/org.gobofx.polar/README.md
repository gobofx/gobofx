# Polar

Rectangular to polar: the frame's left-to-right wraps around the centre as
the angle, the two side edges meeting at twelve o'clock, and top-to-bottom
becomes the radius, the top row at the centre and the bottom row on a rim
half a frame height out. Polar to rectangular undoes it. Beyond the rim the
frame is transparent. At Amount 0 the picture passes through unchanged.

- **Center** — the pole.
- **Amount** — 0 is a no-op, 1 the full warp; in between is a morph.
- **Direction** — rectangular to polar, or polar to rectangular.

## Use it when

- A tiny-planet or a tunnel from a panorama, a skyline or any wide strip
  whose two ends can meet.
- A clock face, a dial, a radar sweep or a circular meter drawn flat and
  wrapped; a flat bar chart becomes a ring.
- Undoing a round graphic to work on it flat, then wrapping it again with
  the other direction.

## Avoid when

- The two side edges do not match: the seam at the top shows as a hard cut
  through the picture.
- A round lens or a swirl is what you mean: use Fisheye, Bulge or Twirl;
  this remaps the whole frame, it does not bend it.
- Anything with a face or text that must read; the top of the frame
  collapses to a point.
