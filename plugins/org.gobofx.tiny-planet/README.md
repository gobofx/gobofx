# Tiny Planet

The stereographic "little planet": the picture is read as a panorama, its
bottom edge gathered into a point at the centre and its top edge thrown out
past the frame's corners, so the ground becomes a small globe under its own
sky. The left and right edges meet on a seam. At Amount 0 the picture passes
through unchanged; the panorama repeats around the planet, so nothing is
pulled from outside the frame.

- **Center** — where the planet sits.
- **Zoom** — the radius of the horizon, in frame heights; smaller makes a smaller planet with more sky.
- **Rotation** — turns the planet around its centre, in degrees; move the seam out of the way with it.
- **Amount** — mix of the planet over the picture; 0 is a no-op.

## Use it when

- A 360° or a wide panoramic shot with the ground along the bottom: a
  skyline, a beach, a field, a stitched drone pan.
- A title or an end card that should sit on its own little world, keyframing
  Rotation slowly so the planet turns.
- An establishing beat that should feel toy-like or storybook rather than
  documentary.

## Avoid when

- The shot has a subject in the middle of the frame; the middle row becomes
  the horizon ring and anything on it bends around the globe.
- The left and right edges do not match; the seam shows as a hard cut
  through the planet unless Rotation hides it behind something.
- A real sphere or a ball is wanted: use Sphere, which keeps the picture
  the right way up on a shaded globe.
