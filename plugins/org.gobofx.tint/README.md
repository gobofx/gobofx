# Tint

A monochrome in one colour: the picture becomes its brightness times the
colour, so white turns into the colour itself and black stays black.
Amount 0 is neutral.

- **Color** — the single colour of the monochrome; white gives a plain
  black-and-white, a dark colour darkens the whole picture with it.
- **Amount** — 0 original, 1 fully tinted.

## Use it when

- A flashback, dream or memory needs one cool or warm cast that reads
  instantly as "elsewhere" when the cut comes.
- A night-for-day or moonlight pass on a layer, with a steel blue and the
  Amount pulled back.
- Branding a picture-in-picture or a background plate in one colour so the
  foreground graphic owns the saturation.

## Avoid when

- The whites should stay white: a tint colours them; Sepia keeps black and
  white in place and colours only the midtones.
- A colour cast on footage that is not meant as a look: Temperature and
  Tint on Color Adjustments correct instead of replacing.
- Skin at partial Amount: half-tinted faces go grey-green before the look
  reads; go all the way or not at all.
