# Colorize

A duotone: the brightness of each pixel picks a point between two colours,
one for the blacks and one for the whites, and the original hues are
discarded. Amount 0 is neutral.

- **Blacks** — the colour black becomes; the default is a deep blue.
- **Whites** — the colour white becomes; the default is a pink.
- **Amount** — 0 original, 1 full duotone.

## Use it when

- A title card, a poster-style still or a section bumper wants two brand
  colours and nothing else.
- Unifying a run of mismatched stock or archive shots under one tone so
  the cut stops jumping.
- Stylising a dark, grainy or low-quality plate: two colours hide a lot of
  noise and compression.

## Avoid when

- Skin matters: two colours give every face the same flat complexion, and
  a cool Blacks colour makes it look ill.
- You want the original colour to survive a little: this discards hue
  entirely; Tint keeps the picture monochrome in one colour, Sepia keeps
  the whites white.
- Highlight and shadow colours that are close in brightness: the picture
  loses its contrast with its hues; keep the Blacks dark and the Whites
  light.
