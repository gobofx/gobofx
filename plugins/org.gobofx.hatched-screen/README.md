# Hatched Screen

The picture as pen crosshatching: parallel ink lines whose weight follows
the tone, with a second, third and fourth set of lines crossing them where
the picture gets darker, the way an engraver builds up shadow. **Amount** 0
is the untouched source.

- **Pitch** — spacing between lines in pixels; 5–8 px reads as an etching, 16+ as a bold graphic.
- **Angle** — direction of the first set of lines in degrees; 45° is the classic hatch, the other sets cross it.
- **Line Count** — how many sets of lines, 1 to 4; one is a line screen, two the classic crosshatch, four a dense engraving.
- **Ink** — the line colour.
- **Paper** — the colour between the lines.
- **Amount** — 0 is the source, 1 the full screen.

## Use it when

- An etched, banknote or woodcut treatment on a portrait, a map or an insert; sepia **Ink** on cream **Paper** sells it.
- A sketchbook or storyboard cutaway where the footage should read as drawn, not photographed.
- A title bed that wants texture with direction, where the halftone dot would look like print rather than hand.

## Avoid when

- Fine detail or text must survive: anything smaller than the pitch dissolves into the lines. Lower **Pitch** or use Edges.
- The frame will be scaled or watched small: the lines alias into moiré at the wrong size.
- You want tone kept as a continuous picture; this replaces every colour with ink and paper. Halftone with a coloured ink is the gentler print.
