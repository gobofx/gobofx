# Gloom

A dark, moody glow: the shadows bleed outward and pull everything near them
down, the inverse of a bloom. Mid-tones beside a dark area sink into it and
the picture turns heavy without losing its highlights. Intensity 0 is a no-op.

- **Threshold** — brightness below which a pixel counts as dark; raise it to pull the mids in too.
- **Radius** — spread in pixels (1 to 64).
- **Intensity** — how far the darks drag their neighbours down; at 1 the darks go to black.

## Use it when

- Horror, noir and thriller grades where the shadows should feel like they
  are creeping into the frame.
- A night interior whose blacks are too clean and need to bleed into the
  lamp-lit walls.
- A memory, a drug sequence or a dream that should feel wrong without any
  overt effect.

## Avoid when

- The shot is already dark and crushed: there is nothing left to pull down
  and the mids go to mud.
- A clean, bright, commercial look; this is the opposite of Bloom and reads
  as deliberate dread.
- A vignette is what is wanted: Gloom follows the picture's own darks, not
  the frame edge.
