# Caustics

The web of light that dances on a pool floor or the underside of a boat,
over transparent. Two layers of cellular ridges whose cells drift in time,
so the bright knots stretch, split and merge like real water.

- **Scale** — cells across the frame height; lower is a bigger, calmer pool.
- **Speed** — how fast the water moves; 0 freezes the web.
- **Intensity** — 0 off, 1 full; above 1 widens the saturated web.
- **Sharpness** — 0 wide soft bands (deep water), 1 thin wires (shallow,
  bright sun).
- **Color** — the light; aqua by default, white for sun on a white hull.

## Use it when

- An underwater or poolside scene was shot without the light play: stack it
  at low Intensity, screen or add blend, masked to surfaces that face up.
- A swimming-pool, aquarium or spa title wants a moving plate behind the
  text.
- A night exterior near water: a blue, slow, sharp web on a wall sells the
  reflection.

## Avoid when

- The scene has no water and no reason to; caustics are one of the most
  recognisable "an effect was added here" tells.
- The surface they land on is not lit from above; a web on a shadowed wall
  reads as a projection.
- Fast speeds with a sharp web: it starts to strobe on long takes.
