# Overdrive

A blown-out glow: the picture is pushed up by a pre-gain before the
highlights are taken, blurred wide, added back and clipped hard at white,
so the brights crush to a flat hot core with a halo around it, like a
camera that has lost its exposure. Intensity 0 is a no-op.

- **Pre-gain** — multiplier on the source before the threshold (1 to 8); higher pulls the mids into the glow.
- **Threshold** — brightness, after the gain, where the glow starts.
- **Radius** — spread in pixels (1 to 64).
- **Intensity** — strength; the result is clipped at white, so more is wider, not brighter.

## Use it when

- A flashback, an overdose, a migraine or a white-out: the picture should
  feel over-exposed and swimming.
- A flash frame or a hit on a beat; keyframe the intensity from 0 and it
  slams to white and back.
- A heavenly or ecstatic beat that Bloom plays too politely.

## Avoid when

- The highlights need to keep detail: they are gone by design; Bloom and
  Glow keep them.
- A clean, naturalistic grade, or anything with a product or a face that
  must still read; skin clips first.
- Every shot of a sequence; one or two frames of overdrive are a punctuation
  mark, a scene of it is a mistake.
