# Broken TV

A tube set losing its signal: a dark hum bar rolls up the picture, and inside
it the rows tear sideways, snow breaks through and red and blue drift apart.
The band is rendered from a 24 fps frame counter, so it animates on its own
and a given time always draws the same frame.

- **Roll Speed** — how many times per second the bar climbs the frame; 0 holds it still.
- **Band Height** — height of the interference bar, as a percentage of the frame.
- **Tear** — how far rows inside the bar slide sideways, in pixels.
- **Noise** — snow inside the bar and the strength of the scanlines everywhere.
- **Color Fringing** — red/blue misconvergence in pixels, faint across the picture and full inside the bar.

## Use it when

- A shot is meant to be seen on a dying set: surveillance footage, a found tape, a transmission that is about to drop.
- A cut to or from archive needs a hit of interference rather than a clean edit.
- A music or title sequence wants a rhythmic glitch; keyframe **Tear** and **Noise** to the beat and leave the roll running.

## Avoid when

- The shot has to stay readable for more than a second or two: the bar hides a third of the frame every pass.
- You want film damage, not video damage: Old Film is the right tool for scratches and gate weave.
- The source is already heavily compressed: the tears and snow compound into mush rather than reading as a signal fault.
