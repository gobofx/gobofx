# Sunburst

Rays radiating from a centre, alternating two colours and slowly turning.
The gap colour defaults to transparent, so the burst stacks over a clip;
make it opaque for a flat poster background.

- **Ray / Gap** — the two colours, with alpha.
- **Center** — where the rays meet.
- **Rays** — how many, 2 to 64.
- **Rotation** — in degrees; **Speed** — turns per second, negative for
  anticlockwise.
- **Softness** — blur of the ray edges, as a fraction of a ray.
- **Falloff** — fades the rays with distance so the centre reads brightest.

## Use it when

- A title card, a sale graphic or a comic-style reveal needs energy behind
  the text: slow rotation, soft edges, a warm ray over a dark plate.
- A sun or lamp in the shot wants exaggerated god rays as a stylised
  overlay, centred on the source and faded with Falloff.
- Retro and vintage looks: a rotating burst behind a product shot is the
  idiom.

## Avoid when

- The footage is naturalistic: a geometric burst over documentary footage
  reads as a sticker.
- The centre sits on a face; the rays converge there and pull the eye to the
  convergence, not the eyes.
- Fast rotation on an opaque burst strobes on screens with lower frame
  rates; keep Speed under a quarter turn per second.
