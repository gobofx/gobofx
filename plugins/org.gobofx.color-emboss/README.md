# Color Emboss

The picture pressed into relief and lit from one side, with its colour
kept: every edge gets a bright lip towards the light and a shadow away from
it, over the original colours rather than a flat grey. **Amount** 0 is the
untouched source.

- **Angle** — where the light comes from, in degrees; 135° lights from the top-left, the convention the eye reads as raised.
- **Height** — depth of the relief in pixels; 1–3 px is a crisp stamp, 6+ a soft bas-relief.
- **Amount** — strength of the light and shadow; 0 is the source, past 2 the edges clip.

## Use it when

- A logo, a title or a flat graphic wants a pressed, embossed-paper feel without losing its colour.
- A subtle sharpening with character: **Height** 1 px and a low **Amount** lifts detail like a print.
- A stylised beat (a stamped letter, a coin, a relief map) where the colour still has to tell the story.

## Avoid when

- Noisy or compressed footage: every block edge becomes a ridge. Blur first or lower **Height**.
- The light direction contradicts the scene's: embossed shadows falling the wrong way look wrong before they look stylised.
- You want the classic grey emboss; keep the colour here, or reach for a monochrome relief when you don't.
