# Halftone

The picture as a printed screen: a grid of ink dots on paper whose size
follows the tone, like a newspaper or a comic. *Mono* is one screen of one
ink; *CMYK* is four screens at the press angles, multiplied like real inks.

- **Dot Size** — screen pitch in pixels, the distance between dot centres.
- **Angle** — screen angle in degrees; 45° is the classic single-ink screen.
- **Mode** — *Mono* for one ink on paper, *CMYK* for a colour print with rotated cyan, magenta, yellow and black plates.
- **Ink** — the dot colour in Mono, and the black plate in CMYK.
- **Paper** — the colour between the dots.

## Use it when

- A pop-art, comic or newsprint look is the brief; CMYK at 10–16 px over a bold image is the poster version.
- A title card or a lower third wants print texture instead of flat colour; try Mono with a coloured ink on an off-white paper.
- You want a stylised cutaway (a flashback, a dream, a "what the papers said") that is unmistakably not the main footage.

## Avoid when

- Fine detail or text has to stay legible: anything smaller than the dot pitch dissolves. Lower **Dot Size** or use Posterize instead.
- The frame will be scaled or shown on a small screen: dots alias into moiré at the wrong size.
- You want grain or texture that keeps the colours; this replaces every colour with ink and paper.
